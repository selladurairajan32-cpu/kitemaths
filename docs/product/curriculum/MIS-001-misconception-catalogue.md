## 1. Catalogue

### Mixing up numerator and denominator

- **Id:** `fractions.numerator-denominator-swap`
- **Skills:** Reading fractions, Showing fractions, Fractions of amounts, Fractions on a number line
- **Example evidence:** Writes the total number of parts on top and the chosen parts underneath, or divides by the numerator.
- **What the child is told (hedged):** It looks like the top and bottom numbers might have swapped places.
- **Recovery explanation:** The **bottom number (denominator)** tells us how many equal parts the whole is split into. Count every part. The **top number (numerator)** tells us how many of those parts we are talking about. Count only the shaded parts.
- **Alternative explanations:** A slip when typing into the boxes (the numbers went in the wrong boxes). Reading the picture from a different side and counting the wrong group.
- **Recovery activity:** guided follow-up questions `fractions.fu-swap-1` and `fractions.fu-swap-2`, then a transfer question: a new question on the same skill (MIS-004).

### Thinking a bigger denominator means a bigger piece

- **Id:** `fractions.bigger-denominator-bigger-piece`
- **Skills:** Comparing fractions, Explaining and spotting mistakes
- **Example evidence:** Judges {1/8} to be larger than {1/3} because 8 is larger than 3.
- **What the child is told (hedged):** You might be thinking a bigger number on the bottom means a bigger fraction.
- **Recovery explanation:** The denominator tells us how many pieces the **same whole** is shared into. Sharing the same whole into **more** pieces makes each piece **smaller**. So with the same numerator, {1/8} is smaller than {1/3}.
- **Alternative explanations:** Comparing the numbers only, without looking at the pictures, as with whole numbers. Thinking about how many pieces there are rather than how big each one is.
- **Recovery activity:** guided follow-up questions `fractions.fu-bd-1` and `fractions.fu-bd-2`, then a transfer question: a new question on the same skill (MIS-004).

### Counting unequal parts as fractions

- **Id:** `fractions.unequal-parts`
- **Skills:** Wholes and equal parts, Explaining and spotting mistakes
- **Example evidence:** Accepts a shape cut into different-sized pieces as showing halves, thirds or quarters.
- **What the child is told (hedged):** It might be that the number of pieces was counted without checking they are the same size.
- **Recovery explanation:** Fractions only work when the whole is split into **equal** parts — fair shares. Three pieces are only thirds if all three pieces are exactly the same size.
- **Alternative explanations:** Counting pieces quickly without checking their sizes. Not seeing a small difference in size in the picture.
- **Recovery activity:** guided follow-up questions `fractions.fu-ue-1` and `fractions.fu-ue-2`, then a transfer question: a new question on the same skill (MIS-004).

### Comparing part to part instead of part to whole

- **Id:** `fractions.part-to-part`
- **Skills:** Reading fractions, Explaining and spotting mistakes
- **Example evidence:** Calls 1 shaded part out of 4 {1/3} because there is 1 shaded and 3 unshaded.
- **What the child is told (hedged):** You may have compared the shaded parts with the unshaded parts, instead of with the whole.
- **Recovery explanation:** The denominator counts **all** the equal parts in the whole — shaded and unshaded together. 1 shaded and 3 unshaded makes 4 parts altogether, so 1 of them is {1/4}.
- **Alternative explanations:** Counting the unshaded parts by mistake. A slip when counting the total.
- **Recovery activity:** guided follow-up questions `fractions.fu-pp-1` and `fractions.fu-pp-2`, then a transfer question: a new question on the same skill (MIS-004).

### Miscounting the total number of parts

- **Id:** `fractions.miscounted-total`
- **Skills:** Reading fractions, Fractions on a number line
- **Example evidence:** Counts lines or tick marks instead of parts or gaps, so the denominator is one too many or too few.
- **What the child is told (hedged):** The total might have been counted a little differently — for example counting the marks instead of the gaps.
- **Recovery explanation:** Count the **parts**, not the lines between them. On a number line, count the **gaps (jumps)** from 0 to 1, not the marks. 5 marks make only 4 gaps.
- **Alternative explanations:** Counting the marks instead of the gaps on a number line. Losing count on a busy picture.
- **Recovery activity:** guided follow-up questions `fractions.fu-mc-1` and `fractions.fu-mc-2`, then a transfer question: a new question on the same skill (MIS-004).

### Finding one part instead of several

- **Id:** `fractions.found-one-part-only`
- **Skills:** Fractions of amounts, Explaining and spotting mistakes
- **Example evidence:** For {3/4} of 12, divides by 4 to get 3 but does not multiply by 3.
- **What the child is told (hedged):** It looks like you found one part, but the question asked for more than one part.
- **Recovery explanation:** Dividing by the **denominator** gives the size of **one** equal part. Then multiply by the **numerator** to collect the number of parts you need.
- **Alternative explanations:** Stopping after the first step by mistake. Misreading the numerator as 1.
- **Recovery activity:** guided follow-up questions `fractions.fu-uo-1` and `fractions.fu-uo-2`, then a transfer question: a new question on the same skill (MIS-004).

### Adding instead of multiplying for equivalence

- **Id:** `fractions.additive-equivalence`
- **Skills:** Equivalent fractions, Explaining and spotting mistakes
- **Example evidence:** Thinks {1/2} = {2/3} because 1 was added to the top and the bottom.
- **What the child is told (hedged):** It might be that the same number was added to the top and bottom, instead of multiplying.
- **Recovery explanation:** Equivalent fractions show the **same amount**. Splitting every part into the same number of smaller parts **multiplies** the top and bottom by the same number. Adding changes the amount.
- **Alternative explanations:** Guessing a pattern from one example. Knowing the rule but slipping in the calculation.
- **Recovery activity:** guided follow-up questions `fractions.fu-add-1` and `fractions.fu-add-2`, then a transfer question: a new question on the same skill (MIS-004).

### Adding or subtracting the denominators

- **Id:** `fractions.added-denominators`
- **Skills:** Adding and subtracting fractions
- **Example evidence:** Writes {2/7} + {3/7} = {5/14}, treating the denominator like another number to add.
- **What the child is told (hedged):** It looks like the bottom numbers might have been added (or taken away) too.
- **Recovery explanation:** The **denominator** names the **size** of the parts: sevenths are all the same size. Adding sevenths to sevenths still gives **sevenths**, so only the numerators are added: {2/7} + {3/7} = {5/7}. The whole is still split into 7.
- **Alternative explanations:** Following the rule for multiplying fractions, which is taught later. A slip: writing the denominator twice.
- **Recovery activity:** guided follow-up questions `fractions.fu-ad-1` and `fractions.fu-ad-2`, then a transfer question: a new question on the same skill (MIS-004).
